# Doctoral Progress Tracker — Student Guide

This is a website that helps you track your progress through your PhD in Rhetoric and Composition: coursework, milestones, semester planning, career goals, mentors, and job market prep. 

You don't need to know anything about coding to use this. This guide walks through everything in plain steps.

## How your information is saved

Everything you type is saved automatically, right in your own browser, on your own device. 

**The one thing to know:** because your progress lives only in your browser, it will *not* follow you if you switch computers, switch browsers, or clear your browser's history/data. Two ways to handle that, both described below: back up your data, or make your own permanent copy of the whole tracker.

## Backing up your progress

On the Milestones & Requirements tab, near the bottom, you'll find two buttons:

- **Export backup (.json)** — downloads a small file with everything you've filled in. Save it somewhere you'll remember (a cloud drive folder is a good option).
- **Import backup** — pick that file to load your progress back in, whether that's on the same computer later or a different one.

A good habit: export a backup every so often, especially before exams, at the end of a semester, or before switching devices.

## Getting your own permanent copy (optional)

If you'd like a version of the tracker that lives under your own name with its own link — for example, to keep using after graduation, or to feel free to heavily customize it — you can make a personal copy through GitHub. This is optional; using the shared link above with regular backups works fine too.

1. Go to [github.com](https://github.com) and create a free account if you don't already have one.
2. Open the tracker's repository page (https://github.com/gabriellawilsonkopko/Composition-and-Rhetoric-Doctoral-Progress-Planner).
3. Click the **Fork** button near the top-right of the page. This creates your own private copy of the whole project under your own account.
4. On your new copy, click **Settings** (top tab), then **Pages** (left sidebar).
5. Under "Build and deployment," set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**, branch **main**, folder **/ (root)**, then **Save**.
6. Wait a minute, then refresh — you'll see your own personal link, something like `https://your-username.github.io/repo-name/`. That's yours to keep, bookmark, and reuse.

Your forked copy starts blank (matching the original template) and is completely separate from anyone else's — nothing you do there affects your program's shared version, or anyone else's copy.

## Turning your progress into a PDF

At the top of the tracker there's a button labeled **Export as PDF**. Click it, and your browser's normal print window will open. Instead of choosing a printer, choose **"Save as PDF"** from the list of destinations, then save. This gives you a clean, nicely formatted PDF of everything you've filled in — handy for sharing a snapshot with your advisor or keeping for your own records.

Tip: if you only want your own career track's requirements in the PDF (not everything for all six tracks), use the track filter on the Milestones tab first, then export.

## Using the tabs

- **Welcome** — the introduction and an overview of the six career tracks.
- **Milestones & Requirements** — everything you're expected to complete, organized by year. Check off your status, add deadlines once you know them, and delete anything that doesn't apply to your program or track (you can always bring deleted items back with the "Restore deleted items" button).
- **Semester Planner** — a term-by-term grid to plan your actual courses and responsibilities.
- **Individual Development Plan (IDP)** — set your own yearly goals; a summary table below fills itself in as you go.
- **Committee & Mentors** — guidance on choosing a committee, plus a place to note who's filling each mentor role for you.
- **Job Market Dossier** — a checklist of materials you'll eventually need for academic job applications.
- **Glossary** — definitions for acronyms and terms used throughout (RCWS, WPA, prospectus, etc.).

## Questions?

This tool is a planning aid, not an official program document. Always check with your advisor and your program's handbook for your institution's actual requirements and deadlines — this is meant to be a starting point for those conversations, not a replacement for them.
